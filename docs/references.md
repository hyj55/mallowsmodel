# References and source attribution

The [estimator inventory](algorithms.md#estimator-inventory-and-references) connects each fitted method to its source, exact algorithm/equation and implementation. A reference for an optimization objective is not automatically a reference for every solver or tuning choice used here.

## Supplied manuscript (M)

The supplied unpublished manuscript, *Selective Mallows Estimation from Uniform Partial Rankings: Minimax Kendall Risk, Efficient Algorithms, and Likelihood Comparisons*, specifies the model, Algorithms 2.1/3.1/3.2, equation (3.4), Lemma 2.11, proof constants (3.22–3.23), and Propositions 4.1–4.3. It is private and is not redistributed. The unpenalized and bounded/regularized experiments implement different specified predictive procedures; neither is described as reproducing every detail of the manuscript by association alone. See the [method contract](algorithms.md).

The [risk-regime comparison](theory_alignment.md) uses the supplied 37-page manuscript, SHA256 `400563b1789acbeea985e4b4c551162b64c87295ee3a7bf91037c8d4cc49e676`, including Theorem 2.1, Propositions 2.2/2.14/2.16, Corollary 2.15, Theorem 2.17, Conjecture 2.18, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.13. The exact version matters because theorem numbering and assumptions are manuscript-specific. The private file is not part of the public repository.

The supplied copy does not identify an author or public publication URL. This repository therefore cites it as **M**, without inventing author metadata or a public link. Its BibTeX record is `selectiveMallowsManuscript` in [references.bib](references.bib). Algorithm 2.1 is on p. 5; the pair sieve is in Lemma 2.11 on pp. 12–13; global Borda is Proposition 2.14 on p. 15 and equation (3.4) on pp. 19–20; the bounded-r global-sieve construction is in the proof of Corollary 2.15 on p. 16; Algorithms 3.1/3.2 are on pp. 19 and 24–25. Algorithm 4.1 and Propositions 4.1/4.2/4.3, on pp. 32–34, supply the predictive protocol, exact DP, dispersion profile and ridge-PL objective respectively.

## Estimators and optimization

1. **Conitzer, V., Davenport, A., & Kalagnanam, J. (2006).** Improved Bounds for Computing Kemeny Rankings. AAAI, 620–626. [Publisher PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2006/AAAI06-099.pdf). The integral version of LP3 on p. 623 supplies the linear-order formulation used for the larger-center benchmark. The repository eliminates reverse-direction variables algebraically. Their experiments used CPLEX 9.1; this project uses HiGHS and does not reproduce that solver's trajectory or runtime. This reference does not supply the repository's multistart-insertion policy.
2. **Hunter, D. R. (2004).** MM algorithms for generalized Bradley–Terry models. *Annals of Statistics*, 32(1), 384–406. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1079120141). Section 5, equation (30): simultaneous, unaccelerated PL MM updates. No added penalty or pseudo-comparisons.
3. **SciPy developers.** [scipy.optimize.milp](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.milp.html) and [minimize(method='L-BFGS-B')](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/optimize.minimize-lbfgsb.html). Solver status, dual bounds and numerical optimization; primary recorded runtime SciPy 1.17.0. Numerical certification is distinguished from an uncertified incumbent. These are solver references, not separate SM/PL statistical estimators.

## Insertion local-search references

**Martí, R., & Reinelt, G. (2011).** *The Linear Ordering Problem: Exact and Heuristic Methods in Combinatorial Optimization*. Springer. [DOI and publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-16729-4), [chapter excerpt](https://beckassets.blob.core.windows.net/product/readingsample/6514515/9783642167287_excerpt_001.pdf). Section 2.3.1, p. 26, defines insertion and explicitly discusses choosing the best move over the entire neighborhood. That rule matches the repository's search step; the separate boxed sequential-scan procedure is not the implementation claimed here. Section 2.4, pp. 30–31, describes multistart. BibTeX key: `marti2011`.

**Schiavinotto, T., & Stützle, T. (2004).** The Linear Ordering Problem: Instances, Search Space Analysis and Algorithms. *Journal of Mathematical Modelling and Algorithms*, 3, 367–402. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:JMMA.0000049426.06305.d8), [paper PDF](https://imada.sdu.dk/u/jbj/heuristikker/Stuetzle.pdf). Section 3, pp. 371–374, defines the insertion neighborhood and move-difference evaluation. Its reported local-search variants use different pivot rules from the repository's full-neighborhood best improvement. This is a reference for the neighborhood, not a claim to reproduce their complete solver or experiments. BibTeX key: `schiavinotto2004`.

These sources establish that insertion local search and multistart are existing methods. The repository supplies its own implementation and declares its initialization, tie handling and integration with integer optimization. The references are methodological support, not a claim of first invention or of code copied from the authors.

## Additional center estimator and background

14. **Fotakis, D., Kalavasis, A., & Stavropoulos, K. (2021).** Aggregating Incomplete and Noisy Rankings. AISTATS, PMLR 130, 2278–2286. [Paper and supplement](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/fotakis21a.html). Section 3, Algorithm 1, p. 2281, supplies PosEst. E0/L0/S0 and G1 require all training pairs to have been observed; C1 uses the same majority-count rule without that admission check. Pair-vote equality counts both directions; final score ties use seeded random priority. Their localized MLE algorithm is not implemented, and M's Theorem 2.17 has a different pair-tie rule.
15. **Mao, C., Weed, J., & Rigollet, P. (2018).** Minimax Rates and Efficient Algorithms for Noisy Sorting. ALT, PMLR 83, 821–847. [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v83/mao18a.html). Independent-pair theory background; it does not justify treating pairs within a report as independent.
16. **Turner, H. L., van Etten, J., Firth, D., & Kosmidis, I. (2020).** Modelling rankings in R: the PlackettLuce package. *Computational Statistics*, 35, 1027–1057. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-020-00959-3), [official overview](https://hturner.github.io/PlackettLuce/articles/Overview.html). Data/package context; P1/P2 use unpenalized Python MM, while explicitly named regularization experiments use ridge PL; the package's default pseudo-rankings are not substituted for either.

## Optimization, sports and inference references

25. **Raman, K., & Joachims, T. (2014).** Methods for Ordinal Peer Grading. *Proceedings of KDD*, 1037–1046. [Author PDF](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/people/tj/publications/raman_joachims_14a.pdf). Section 3.1.1 and Algorithm 2 (PDF pp. 3–4) supply C1's greedy MAL center heuristic. The implementation fixes every assessor reliability to one, uses strict rankings, and supplies a seeded tie rule. The paper's text calls the procedure a greedy approximation; C1 does not claim exact MLE, implement its reliability-learning extension, or use its score-weighted MALS or MALBC variants. BibTeX key: `raman2014`.
26. **Cantwell, G. T., & Moore, C. (2022).** Belief propagation for permutations, rankings, and partial orders. *Physical Review E*, 105, L052303. Player, tournament and completed-match source for T1; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 attribution is retained.
28. **Efron, B. (1979).** Bootstrap Methods: Another Look at the Jackknife. *Annals of Statistics*, 7(1), 1–26. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552). Empirical-distribution resampling rationale; it does not by itself justify treating unidentified dependent rows as independent units.
